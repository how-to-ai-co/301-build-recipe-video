# Mad Libs — build on a structure that already worked

**The fastest way to a good ad is not a blank page.** Take an ad that already
went viral, keep its beat structure and timings exactly, and swap in your own
subject. The rhythm is the format; the content is the variable.

Below is one worked example end to end: a well-known screen-time-app ad,
studied frame by frame, broken into its beats, and turned into a
fill-in-the-blank template you complete for your own brand.

**How to use this with an operator:**

1. Ask what they want the ad to be about.
2. Find the beat structure that fits, or use the one below.
3. Fill the blanks WITH them, out loud, one beat at a time. Do not write the
   whole thing and present it.
4. Then route the finished script into a format: `OneWord`, `Cards`, or a
   generated-footage cut.

**Do not copy the source ad's footage, script or shots.** The structure is the
reusable part. The words and pictures have to be yours. That also means: watch
the source ad yourself before using its template. A template built from a
description is weaker than one built from the timings you verified on your own
screen.

# Worked example: the Opal screen-time ad

Source: https://www.instagram.com/p/C0cF7LQNqL4/ (Opal screen-time app, 51s,
9:16, faceless). Study it frame by frame yourself with a stopwatch before
building on it; the beat map below is a paraphrase, not a transcript.

## 1. What the voiceover does, beat by beat

The exact words belong to the source ad. What you are borrowing is the moves:

| Time | The move |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:01 | Denies that the mundane object on screen is what you think it is |
| 0:01–0:06 | Reframes it as something enormous and menacing, with a giant number attached, aimed at the viewer personally |
| 0:07–0:14 | Explains the mechanism: the viewer's own impulses are being exploited to drain a precious resource |
| 0:14–0:21 | Borrows a named authority, points to where you can verify them, then promises the bottom line |
| 0:21–0:25 | Delivers the despair floor in blunt second person, then pivots to hope |
| 0:28–0:41 | States a universal truth about the resource, then asks how much of it the viewer surrenders |
| 0:43–0:51 | Says the viewer can take control but must fight for it, then commands them to leave the very platform they are watching on |

## 2. Beat-by-beat visual map

1. **Cold open, 0–1s.** Real footage of the mundane object (a phone, in a
   store). VO denies it is what it looks like.
2. **The reframe barrage, 1–9s.** About one cut per second: a headline
   screenshot carrying the giant number, technical cutaway diagrams, archival
   footage, medical imagery. Every cut is *evidence* for the scary reframe.
3. **"This is about YOU," 9–14s.** A person doomscrolling in bed, a real
   screenshot of the viewer's own damning stat, a scroll over a shoulder, an
   extreme closeup of an eye with the feed reflected in it. The viewer sees
   themselves.
4. **Authority borrow, 14–21s.** Real footage of a named expert testifying,
   their key phrase as a huge caption overlay, a name card. Credibility on
   loan, no host needed.
5. **Gut-punch title cards, 20–25s.** Black screens, white type: a "bottom
   line" card, a visceral second-person card, a no-one-is-coming card. Then
   the turn: a hope-pivot card.
6. **The physical metaphor, 25–42s.** Tabletop, gloved hands, coins standing
   in for hours. Coins sorted into labeled jars for the things the viewer
   actually wants, and one jar stuffed to the brim, plastered with the
   villain's logos, while the others sit nearly empty.
7. **The smash, 43–48s.** Hammer through the villain's jar, slow-mo glass and
   coins everywhere. Catharsis, which is the product's job, shown physically.
8. **Anti-CTA + product reveal, 49–51s.** A title card commanding the viewer
   to leave the platform. Cut to the actual product screen doing exactly that.
   Logo. End.

**Why it works:** mundane object → menacing reframe with rapid-fire
"evidence" → mirror held up to the viewer → borrowed authority → despair floor
→ hope pivot → a physical, tactile metaphor for an abstract cost → violent
catharsis → an anti-CTA that *is* the product demo. The ad never asks you to
buy; it tells you to leave, and the product is how you leave.

## 3. The Mad Libs template

Fill every [BLANK]. Keep the beat lengths; the rhythm is the format.

> **BEAT 1 — Cold open (0–1s).** Footage of [MUNDANE OBJECT the audience
> touches daily]. VO: one line denying the object is what it looks like.
>
> **BEAT 2 — Reframe barrage (1–8s).** VO: the object is really [GIANT NUMBER]
> [MENACING REFRAME] aimed at the viewer's [VULNERABLE THING]. Visuals: 6–8
> rapid cuts of real-looking evidence: [HEADLINE SCREENSHOT], [TECHNICAL
> DIAGRAM], [ARCHIVAL FOOTAGE], [SCIENTIFIC IMAGERY].
>
> **BEAT 3 — Mirror (8–14s).** VO: names [THE MECHANISM: how the villain
> exploits the viewer's own impulses] and [THE PRECIOUS RESOURCE it drains].
> Visuals: someone who looks like the viewer doing the guilty behavior, plus
> one undeniable screenshot receipt ([THE VIEWER'S OWN DAMNING STAT]).
>
> **BEAT 4 — Authority borrow (14–20s).** VO: quotes [AUTHORITY'S KEY PHRASE],
> names [AUTHORITY NAME], points to [WHERE TO VERIFY], promises the bottom
> line. Visuals: real footage of the authority, name card, key phrase as giant
> type.
>
> **BEAT 5 — Gut punch (20–25s).** Black title cards, white type: a bottom-line
> card, then "[VISCERAL SECOND-PERSON CARD]", then a no-one-is-coming card.
> Then the hope pivot card.
>
> **BEAT 6 — Physical metaphor (25–41s).** VO: [UNIVERSAL TRUTH about the
> precious resource], then asks how much of it goes to [THE VILLAIN]. Visuals:
> [TANGIBLE PROP] = [ABSTRACT RESOURCE], sorted into labeled containers; the
> villain's container overflows, the containers labeled [WHAT THE VIEWER
> ACTUALLY WANTS] sit empty.
>
> **BEAT 7 — Catharsis (41–48s).** [DESTRUCTION OR LIBERATION of the villain's
> container], slow motion.
>
> **BEAT 8 — Anti-CTA (48–52s).** Title card: "[COMMAND THAT SOUNDS AGAINST
> YOUR OWN INTEREST]." Cut to [PRODUCT SCREEN doing exactly that command].
> Logo.

## 4. Filling it in for your brand

Do this as a worksheet with the operator, one blank per line, before any shot
is written. Every value below is a placeholder to replace, not copy to keep:

- [MUNDANE OBJECT] → the everyday thing your audience touches that connects to
  your product's problem
- [GIANT NUMBER + MENACING REFRAME] → a real, sourceable number; put its
  receipt (the headline screenshot) on screen in Beat 2
- [PRECIOUS RESOURCE] → what your audience is actually losing (hours, money,
  attention, standing)
- [VISCERAL SECOND-PERSON CARD] → the bluntest true sentence about the
  viewer's situation; it must sting for a beat before the hope pivot
- [TANGIBLE PROP] → something physical that can stand in for the resource,
  countable and sortable into containers
- [CATHARSIS] → the villain's container destroyed or the viewer's container
  rescued
- [ANTI-CTA] → a command that appears to work against your own interest but
  demonstrates the product's promise

### Two ways to deploy the finished script

**Organic first.** Keep the video clean: no product screen, no offer inside
the video. The subscribe or purchase invite lives in the caption only, so the
video earns shares on its own. Organic tests the hook cheap.

**Paid second.** If the organic cut performs, the paid variant is a small
diff: swap the Beat 7 or Beat 8 VO to the offer line and end on the offer
screen. Paid scales the winner. One concept, two offers, one number in the
naming convention.

### Segment swaps

The whole script stays fixed; only two lines change per audience segment. That
keeps the test clean and the production cost near zero:

| Segment | Beat 2 aim | Beat 3 receipt |
|---|---|---|
| [SEGMENT 1] | "…aimed at [what this segment protects]" | [screenshot this segment would recognize] |
| [SEGMENT 2] | "…aimed at [what this segment protects]" | [screenshot this segment would recognize] |
| [NICHE SEGMENT] | keep the generic aim | [one niche-specific headline as a Beat 2 cut] |

### Production notes

- Fully faceless is achievable: macro phone shots, stock and archival cuts,
  title cards, tabletop props. The container scene is one afternoon tabletop
  shoot or a generated sequence (hands plus tabletop is within range of the
  video models in `parts/50` and `parts/60`).
- Every number on screen needs its receipt on screen or in the caption. A
  claim that cannot survive comment-section scrutiny costs more than it earns.
- No profanity if the cut will ever run as a paid ad; platform review will
  bounce it. Let the anti-CTA card do the shock work instead.
- The anti-CTA must genuinely sting for a beat before any resolve card. Do not
  soften it by showing the logo early.
- Check the finished script against your brand compliance list before posting.
  Example of the kind of rule that belongs on such a list, anonymized from the
  source project: a "leave this platform" card posted on that same platform
  needed explicit brand-owner sign-off, because the irony is the point and the
  brand owner has to own it.
